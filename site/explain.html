03 / IMPLEMENTED BENCH LOGIC

Explain one complete working cycle.

Acquire

Read paired air sensors, roof temperature, water contact, fan rotation and fan-branch power about once per second.

Validate & decide

Reject missing or invalid readings. Manual mode commands 70% duty. Automatic mode is disabled by default; its optional 32°C bench trigger is not a safety threshold.

Act locally

Drive the fan PWM interface, update the OLED and latch local assistance. Tach feedback can flag a rotation fault after startup grace.

Record & compare

Send timestamped serial records to the laptop. CoolProof requires valid reference, timing, calibration and coverage before allowing a comparison conclusion.

04 / CURRENT GAP REVIEW

Clear explanations. Honest unfinished work.

Presentation gaps addressed

The original architecture was hidden in a collapsed section. Selecting ESP32 showed only its size, while the board stayed hidden inside the enclosure. The new visible block diagram, component rationale, cutaway, search and tour connect the explanation to the CAD.

Electronics still to detail

The discrete PWM/buzzer drivers, fuse, supply, connectors, full wire harness and standalone power conversion are not detailed CAD parts. They are explicitly shown as electrical requirements in the diagram. Full-size cable interfaces and outdoor actuator selection remain open.

Sensor placement still to validate

The “reference” sensor is near the roof overhang and is not yet a valid external control. Shields are simplified envelopes. The roof probe needs a thermal contact fixture. Placement rationale is a design hypothesis, not a measurement result.

Manufacturing & cost still open

Foundation, structural connections, roof weatherproofing, enclosure sealing and supplier fit need professional review. Current full-size allowance is about ₹94,000–₹1.71 lakh, unquoted; the below-installed-AC target is not demonstrated.

05 / SPEAKING NOTES

Questions your panel may ask.

Why two air sensors?

One samples the occupied zone, the other is intended for comparison. Both need matched shielding, calibration and appropriate placement. Temperature alone cannot demonstrate fan benefit; useful air speed needs a separate instrument.

Why put the controller inside, but air sensors outside the enclosure?

The enclosure protects electronics and supports service access. Its heat and trapped air would bias environmental readings, so the air sensors sit in dedicated shields. The metal enclosure also means wireless performance cannot be assumed.

Why a double roof and louvres?

They test shade and ventilation mechanisms inspired by climate-responsive architecture. Their effectiveness depends on sun direction, wind, surface properties and construction. We will measure those effects rather than promise a temperature drop.

Why not claim the fan proves cooling?

Tachometer rotation is an actuator-health signal. Useful air speed, radiant conditions, humidity and human responses are different measurements. No occupational safety claim is made.

Where is the AI?

An optional local model can explain finished experiment records. It is read-only, disabled by default, and not connected to the public demo. Deterministic firmware handles control; AI cannot declare a person safe.

Is this a connected IoT product already?

The implemented path is ESP32 → USB serial → laptop bridge → local API/database → dashboard. Internet hosting currently publishes the demonstration. Wi-Fi/cloud device telemetry is a future integration.

What is novel?

The proposal combines a repairable shelter with traceable comparison records and explicit invalid-result handling. Novelty remains a research question; this work does not establish a world-first invention or patentability.

What is the next physical experiment?

Assemble the unoccupied tabletop bench, calibrate sensors, test disconnections and fan faults, then run repeated paired trials with swapped positions. Record airflow, roof response, energy, uncertainty and exclusions. Miniature results do not directly predict full-size results.
